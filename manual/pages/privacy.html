Scissor has no server behind your work. The engine runs inside the browser tab, and so does everything it does: drawing, editing, importing SVG, PDF, .ai and PSD files, and exporting. Your artwork is never uploaded, unless you choose to attach it to a problem report (see below).

Where your documents live

Documents are files on your own computer. Open and Save use the browser's own file picker, so you choose the folder and Scissor writes back to the file you picked. In browsers without that picker, saving and exporting arrive as ordinary downloads and opening goes through a file chooser. The native document format is .cut.

Recovery after a crash

While a document has unsaved changes, Scissor writes a copy into the browser's own storage on this machine every few seconds, and again whenever the tab is hidden or closed. If a session ends badly, the next time you open Scissor you are offered the leftover work with a Recover button. Saving a document clears its copy. In a private window, or where site storage is blocked, recovery is unavailable and nothing is written.

Analytics

Visits are counted by country at the edge of the network, by Cloudflare, with nothing added to the page and no cookies. Scissor can also use Google Analytics, which sets its own cookies, to count visits and see which features are used. In the EU, the EEA, the UK, Switzerland, Brazil, Canada, Turkey, South Korea, China, India and South Africa it stays off unless you allow it: on a later visit, after a few minutes of use, a bar at the foot of the window asks once, and your answer is remembered. Elsewhere it is on by default. Anywhere, you can switch it on or off in Preferences > Privacy; turning it off reloads the page so the tracking code is gone. Either way your artwork stays on your computer.

Studios

A Studio lets people work on one file together, see each other's pointers and talk while they work. Scissor's server only introduces the people in a Studio to each other: it keeps their display names and who is in which Studio in memory while the Studio is open, and stores nothing. After that, everyone in the Studio connects to everyone else directly, so they can see each other's IP address. The shared file, every change to it and the pointers are sent directly between you, never through the server, and everyone in the Studio ends up with a copy of the file. Voice is off until you turn it on, and your browser asks before Scissor can use the microphone; your voice also goes directly to the others and is not recorded. A Studio ends ten minutes after the last person leaves. The host can lock a Studio so that newcomers wait until they are let in, and can mute people.

Problem reports

Help > Report a Problem sends a report to Scissor's own server only when you press Send Report, and only after you agree to it in the form. The report holds what you write and the Scissor version, plus whatever you tick: your browser and system, a screenshot of the canvas, the steps you recorded, and the document, which is never sent unless you tick it. If you give your email address, it is used only to reply about your report. A person reads each report to fix the problem, and reports are never shared or sold. Screenshots and documents are deleted after 30 days, and the rest of the report after a year. To stop the form from being flooded, your IP address is used for a few minutes to limit how many reports can be sent, and it is not stored with the report. To have a report deleted sooner, ask on the public issue tracker and quote the reference the form showed you.

Installing the app

Choose Help > Install Scissor… to install Scissor as an app. It then opens in its own window, works without a connection because the browser has kept a copy of the files, and can open .cut, SVG, PDF, .ai and PSD files directly from your desktop. There is no account and no sign-in.
